# Passages

## Mission, Theory of Change & Principles

*November 21, 2026*

## Mission and vision

**Mission**

Passages helps people discover literature through the writing itself, one passage at a time, and makes it easier to begin reading.

**Vision**

A world in which great literature, from every language and tradition, feels within reach of anyone curious enough to read a page.

Passages is for people who can read but don't often read literature: someone who has always wanted to try Dostoyevsky but doesn't know where to begin; someone who has never found a poet they love; or someone who rarely encounters literature from their own language and tradition.

The aim isn't to tell people what they ought to read. It's to help them find something they want to read.

## The problem

Literature is widely available, but that doesn't mean people can find their way into it.

Thousands of classic works are in the public domain and cost nothing to read. Yet many people never begin them. The obstacle isn't always availability or price. Often, it's everything that comes before the first page.

**Discovery.** Books are usually encountered through covers, reviews, bestseller lists, recommendations, and popularity-driven algorithms. These can be useful, but they don't necessarily help someone discover a writer through the quality of their writing.

**Intimidation.** Some works arrive with a reputation that precedes them. A long novel, an unfamiliar author, or a book described as difficult can feel like an obligation rather than an invitation. People may assume that certain literature isn't meant for them.

**The commitment of starting.** It's difficult to know whether you'll enjoy a 600-page novel before you've read a page of it. Choosing a book can feel like making a much bigger commitment than simply discovering a passage.

**Limited horizons.** Readers are often introduced to a narrow selection of authors, languages, and literary traditions. Much of the world's literature remains unfamiliar, even to people who love reading.

Passages starts with a simple idea: let people encounter the writing before they have to decide whether to read the work.

## How Passages hopes to make a difference

The central idea is that a passage can do something a description, review, or recommendation cannot: let someone experience the writing for themselves.

A reader discovers a passage. It catches their attention. They can learn a little about the work and its author, then begin reading the complete work without having to leave the experience or find another source.

The passage is not meant to replace the work. It is an invitation into it.

The experience follows a simple path:

1. **Discover.** A reader encounters a passage from a novel, short story, poem, or other literary work. The writing takes center stage.
2. **Explore.** If something catches their attention, they can learn more through a short, spoiler-free introduction to the work and the passage.
3. **Read.** They can begin the work, ideally from the beginning, in the same place.
4. **Continue.** When they reach the passage that first drew them in, they encounter it again in its original context.
5. **Discover further.** Over time, they may find themselves exploring unfamiliar authors, periods, languages, and literary forms.

Public-domain works are a natural starting point. They let Passages offer complete reading experiences without asking readers to pay for access. But the ambition is broader than the classics or the public domain.

This is a theory to test, not an assumption to take for granted. The pilot should help answer a few questions:

- Can a short passage create a genuine desire to read a complete work?
- Does removing the friction between discovering a work and starting it make people more likely to read?
- Can a literary feed encourage people to leave the feed and spend their time with the writing itself?
- Can it introduce readers to works and traditions they might not otherwise encounter?

## What impact looks like

The most important question is whether Passages helps people read works they might otherwise never have started.

That means measuring what happens after discovery, not simply how much time someone spends in the app.

| Measure | What it helps us understand |
| --- | --- |
| **Works started** | How often curiosity turns into someone beginning a work |
| **Continued reading** | Whether people who start a work keep going, rather than simply opening it |
| **Passage interest** | Which passages prompt readers to learn more about a work |
| **Reader confidence** | Whether people feel more comfortable approaching literature they once found intimidating |
| **Breadth of discovery** | Whether readers explore unfamiliar authors, languages, traditions, and forms |

The first three can be explored through anonymous, aggregate usage data. The last two will require conversations with readers.

For the initial pilot, a useful starting measure is the proportion of people who open a work's introduction and go on to start reading it. Reaching the second chapter, or an equivalent point in a shorter work, can offer an early indication that they continued.

These measures are imperfect. Starting a work doesn't mean someone will finish it, and a saved passage doesn't tell us exactly why it mattered. Interviews and reader feedback will help put the numbers in context.

Some things are deliberately not measures of success: time spent in the feed, number of swipes, or daily app opens. If someone discovers a passage, leaves the app after two minutes, and spends the evening reading Chekhov, that is a success.

## Principles

These principles should guide Passages as it grows. They matter just as much when making decisions about funding, partnerships, technology, and revenue as they do when designing the product.

**1. Access comes first.**

Public-domain works should remain freely discoverable and readable. The core experience should not depend on a subscription or a reader's ability to pay. Where other works are included, access should be pursued in ways that respect authors and rights holders.

**2. Reading matters more than scrolling.**

The feed is a way into literature, not an end in itself. The product should make it easy to leave the feed and start reading. Passages has no reason to compete for someone's attention once it has helped them find something worth reading.

**3. Literature should be broad and representative.**

A literary collection should not simply reproduce what is already popular or familiar. Passages should make room for different languages, cultures, periods, traditions, and forms, including writers who have received less attention.

**4. Editorial judgment remains human.**

Technology can help discover, organize, and evaluate candidate passages. It cannot replace the responsibility of deciding what belongs in the collection, understanding its context, and presenting it thoughtfully. Editorial decisions remain accountable to people.

**5. Readers deserve privacy.**

Passages should collect only the information it needs to understand and improve the experience. Reader data should not be sold, and participation should not come at the expense of privacy.

**6. The mission should guide growth.**

Funding, partnerships, and new features should help Passages make literature more accessible. Growth is useful only insofar as it serves that purpose.

## Where things stand

Passages is currently an independent project with a working prototype. It is an early attempt to see whether a different approach to literary discovery can help more people find their way into reading.

The immediate priorities are modest:

- **Test the experience.** Put Passages in front of readers, particularly people who don't regularly read classics, poetry, or literary fiction. Find out what draws them in, what puts them off, and whether they go on to read.
- **Build a literary community around the project.** Seek the advice of librarians, literature scholars, editors, translators, educators, and people working in public humanities.
- **Improve the collection.** Develop a thoughtful, transparent editorial process that gives readers a meaningful introduction to a wide range of literature.
- **Learn what kind of support is possible.** Explore partnerships, grants, donations, and other forms of support that could help the project become sustainable.

The right long-term structure is still an open question. Passages might become a nonprofit, operate under a fiscal sponsor, or take another mission-driven form. There is no need to settle that now. The important thing is to find a way to sustain the work without compromising its purpose.

**Passages begins with a simple belief: people are more likely to read something when they have the chance to fall in love with the writing first.**
